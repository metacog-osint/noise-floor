From the daily pipeline, backfilled to 1 October 2025: automatic and not reviewed by a person, apart from the 20 validated days. A new day is added each morning. Live counts (aircraft degraded after leaving interference elsewhere are left out). The same measure and areas as the validation chart further down, plus Cyprus–Levant and the Strait of Hormuz / Gulf of Oman (added 22 Oct 2026; little ADS-B coverage over Iran, so it mostly measures the Omani and UAE side and the airways over water). All panels share one scale, so levels compare directly.

Hollow dots mark days when an area had under half its usual aircraft-hexes (median of its previous 14 days). Strong jamming can stop aircraft sending a position at all, so they drop out instead of counting as degraded, and the share on those days comes from the aircraft that kept a position. On 9 May 2026 three in four aircraft crossing the southern Baltic lost their ADS-B position (validation notes, verdict 34). The map's "Fewer aircraft than usual" view shows where.

Which aircraft types carry the effect downstream

Over-representation among aircraft still degraded after leaving interference

Types with at least 40 post-exposure aircraft-days. A350s and A380s are shown apart, in grey: their high ratios most likely reflect how they report accuracy (they flag it as poor when a single satellite is marked degraded, and stay degraded longer; Validation), not lasting harm. Among the other types, long-haul widebodies stand out, which points to route exposure on the Gulf and Asia corridors.
