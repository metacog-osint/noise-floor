How to read these charts

The first chart updates every morning from the automatic daily run. The rest are tests from the validation report, drawn from the processed aircraft data. Hover or use the arrow keys for values; every chart also has a table.

The daily chart. From the daily pipeline, backfilled to 1 October 2025: automatic and not reviewed by a person, apart from the 20 validated days. A new day is added each morning. Aircraft still degraded after leaving interference elsewhere are left out. The same measure and areas as the validation chart further down, plus Cyprus–Levant and the Strait of Hormuz / Gulf of Oman (added 22 Oct 2026; little ADS-B coverage over Iran, so it mostly measures the Omani and UAE side and the airways over water).

Hollow dots mark days when an area had under half its usual aircraft (median of its previous 14 days). Strong jamming can stop aircraft sending a position at all, so they drop out instead of counting as degraded, and the share on those days comes from the aircraft that kept a position. On 9 May 2026 three in four aircraft crossing the southern Baltic lost their ADS-B position (validation notes, verdict 34). The map's "Fewer aircraft than usual" view shows where.

The spoofing chart. A spoofed receiver is told it is somewhere else. Noisefloor finds this from positions an aircraft can't have flown: near-zero or very low ground speed at cruise height, staying in one spot at altitude, or a jump to a separate track and back. An episode is "active" when another aircraft was spoofed in the same region (about 300 km across) within an hour, as real interference affects many aircraft at once; one-off cases are left out of these counts. The regional panels count cells, each about 1,800 km², where spoofed aircraft really were during active episodes: an area measure, not a count of aircraft. Regions are the validation's boxes (the Middle East, the Black Sea, the Baltic and western Russia, South Asia); everything else, including central and eastern Europe, is under Elsewhere. The detector changed to version 0.6 on 15 Nov 2026 and the days from 7 Nov were redone with it, so the line steps there partly because of the method (v0.6 validation).

Places receivers were told they were. Each day's false positions are grouped into places (points within 5 km), and a place is followed from day to day while its centre stays within 10 km. Aircraft-days add each day's aircraft, so one aircraft seen on two days counts twice. "Aircraft really were" names the regions where most of those aircraft actually flew. A place is new when 3 or more aircraft were shown there and nothing was shown within 10 km on the earlier days (up to 30) with these files, which start on 7 Nov 2026. Spoofing points in the Gulf move often, so most new places there are a known point that moved; a new place thousands of km from its aircraft, as with Lima in Nov 2026, is rarer. Each place also has a usual level: the median number of aircraft shown there a day over up to 30 earlier days with these files, counting 0 on days it wasn't used, once 7 such days exist. "Above" means at least twice that and 3 or more aircraft over it; "below" means half or less and 3 or more under it. The line above the table says the same for all places together. It compares a place with its own normal; it is not a warning. A false position is where receivers were told they were, not where a transmitter is.
