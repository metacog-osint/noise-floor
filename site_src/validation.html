A finding: downstream effects by aircraft type

Over 17 days, A350-900s were 24.9% of aircraft-days still degraded after leaving interference, against 1.3% of traffic (19.4×). A380s were 19.2× and 777-300ERs 4.7×; most narrowbodies were near 1×. 777-300ERs fly the same long-haul corridors as A350s, so aircraft type seems to matter beyond route. This is a hypothesis: one possibility is avionics rejecting GPS for the rest of a flight after spoofing exposure. Airbus and EASA guidance hasn't been checked yet.

Validation still to come

  • Published localizations, such as the 2024 Baltic open-source work, scored as known positives.
  • Announced test events (Jammertest in Norway, US GPS interference test notices) as known positives and planned-test exclusions.
  • Known false look-alikes, such as radar faults, as cases the methods must not flag.
  • A surveyed emitter or announced test to measure NISAR position accuracy.

Each case is checked against its source before it's scored.
