# How to read our judgments

Every program of The Intelligence Company keeps two things apart: what we **measured** and what we **judge** it means.

- **A measurement** (an anomaly) says something is outside its normal range, for example "severe GPS error over the
  eastern Gulf of Finland, 41% of aircraft against a usual 6%". It is not a warning.
- **A judgment** (a warning, assessment or forecast) says what we think is happening or will happen. It always carries
  a likelihood, a separate confidence level, the alternatives we considered, and what would change our mind.

## Likelihood: how probable we think it is

We store a number and show the word with its range. The words and ranges are those of US Intelligence Community
Directive 203. The UK Defence Intelligence equivalent appears when you hover over or tap the word.

| Word | Range | UK equivalent |
|---|---|---|
| Almost no chance | 01-05% | Remote chance |
| Very unlikely | 05-20% | Highly unlikely |
| Unlikely | 20-45% | Unlikely |
| Roughly even chance | 45-55% | Realistic possibility (no exact equivalent) |
| Likely | 55-80% | Likely or probable |
| Very likely | 80-95% | Highly likely |
| Almost certain | 95-99% | Almost certain |

Where a number falls on a shared edge (for example exactly 20%), we round towards the middle of the scale.

## Confidence: how sure we are of the evidence

Confidence is about the evidence behind a judgment, not about the likelihood. We never put the two in one sentence. It is
**computed by fixed rules, not by statistics**:

- **High:** three or more independent lines of evidence agree, with nothing contradicting them, few gaps or
  assumptions, evidence that is hard to fake, alternatives examined and rejected, and a method with a scored track
  record. High confidence is still not certainty.
- **Moderate:** two or more independent lines agree, but something keeps it short of high.
- **Low:** one line of evidence, old or patchy data, or a weak source.

Each judgment lists what would raise its confidence. The full rules are in [the confidence rubric](confidence-rubric.md).

## Grey means no data

When a source stops or covers too little of an area, the map shows grey and says "no data". Silence from a feed never
means quiet.

## Who's who: how we describe sides

We name the actors in an event and say what part each plays, the way our sources describe it: a party to a conflict,
a supporter of a party, the one held responsible, those affected, civilians, or a third party such as a mediator or
monitor. We never label anyone friendly, hostile or an enemy. Sides are named the way the sources name them.

Every description says how we know it: **assessed** (our own method, or several independent sources), **reported** (an
independent outlet or agency), or **claimed** (one party or one source; drawn hollow, and never enough on its own to
move a warning). Where it matters, we show how each side's media is telling the same story.

## How we keep score

Every forecast is logged before the event, with a written rule for deciding whether it came true. Verdict scores them
all, misses included.

## What we claim

Our methods are aligned with the principles of Intelligence Community Directives 203, 206 and 208. Those directives bind
US intelligence agencies only. We are an independent, non-commercial research effort using public information, so we
do not claim compliance.
