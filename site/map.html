A350/A380: the share of an event's degraded aircraft that were A350s or A380s. These types flag their accuracy as poor far more readily than others and stay degraded longer, so an event resting mostly on them (shown in bold, 50% or more) is weaker evidence of interference on its own. Inside real jamming they are also among the best witnesses, because they keep reporting while other types go dark. Recorded from 25 Oct 2026; earlier days show "–". Method

How these are found
  • Anomaly: an H3 resolution-2 region (about 87,000 km²) in one UTC hour where the share of degraded aircraft is well above the same hour on up to 14 earlier days. It needs 50 or more aircraft-cell-hours, a binomial z of 4 or more against the usual rate, and an excess of at least 2 percentage points (93 m) or 10 points (10 m) and 4 times the usual spread. Neighbouring regions and hours join into one event.
  • Level is the largest excess in the event: major 25 points or more, notable 10–25, minor under 10. "Peak share" is the highest share in any of its region-hours.
  • Burst: 3 or more minutes in which the number of aircraft with a severe-error report in a region rises above its usual level for that 15-minute slot on earlier days (at least 5 aircraft, and 5 times the usual spread). Short, sharp events show here: tests, short-lived jammers, the start of a spoofing episode.
  • Aircraft still degraded after leaving interference elsewhere are left out of both. With fewer than 5 earlier days there are no anomalies, and bursts are judged against the day's own level.
  • Announced tests: US GPS interference tests announced in the FAA's public flight advisories. An event is tagged when it overlaps a test window and lies within the test's widest radius (the one for aircraft at FL400). The dashed circle shows the radius at FL250. A tag means the event fits an announced test, not that the test caused it.
  • Automatic and not reviewed by a person. An event is an effect, not attribution: storms, SBAS problems and avionics faults can cause them too. Code: pnt_analysis.py.

How to read this map

The share of aircraft in each cell whose own GPS accuracy degraded that day, from their ADS-B position-quality reports. It shows effects, not who caused them. A new day appears each morning: adsb.lol publishes each UTC day at about 03:30 UTC the next day, and the map updates by about 06:00 UTC (1 am CDT, midnight CST). The map covers every day since 1 October 2025: use ‹ and › beside the day, pick a day from the list (notable days first), or click a bar in the strip under the map. Daily runs are automatic and not reviewed by a person; the 20 validated test days are marked.

  • Click a cell to see the history of the area around it (an H3 resolution-3 cell, about 12,000 km²): its share for every day since 1 October 2025, with the days an anomaly event covered it. Click a point on that chart to open the day.
  • Satellite imagery (Map options → Base map → Satellite imagery) puts Esri World Imagery under the cells: quiet cells turn faint, the coloured ones stay strong, and the map zooms closer (to zoom 10) to show what lies under a hotspot. It is offered only where Esri's terms allow (see the terms of use) and stops if the site's free monthly allowance runs out.
  • Change from usual colours each cell by how far today's share sits above its own median over its previous 14 days with traffic. Persistent hotspots fade, so new or growing activity stands out. Cells with fewer than 5 such days show as low coverage.
  • Fewer aircraft than usual compares each cell's aircraft count with its median over the previous 14 days. Strong jamming can stop aircraft sending a position at all, and then they vanish from the map instead of showing as degraded: on 9 May 2026 three in four aircraft crossing the southern Baltic lost their ADS-B position (validation notes, verdict 34). Weekends, holidays, airspace closures and receiver outages lower counts too, so read it alongside the other views. Try 2026-05-09.
  • Spoofing (the Spoofing button above the map, or Map options → Measure → Spoofing) turns off the GPS-error colours and shades each region about 300 km across by the share of its aircraft-hours spent in active spoofing episodes, counted where the aircraft really were. A bold dashed outline marks a region above its own usual level: at least twice its median over up to 30 earlier days, and 5 or more spoofed aircraft-hours over it. That compares the region with its own normal; it is not a warning. Crosses mark where receivers were told they were. Hover a region to see where its aircraft were shown; click it to draw lines there. "Pool the last 7 days" adds up the week, which settles single-day noise in regions with little traffic. The table under the map then lists the regions with the most spoofing.
  • Limb S4 (Map options → Space weather): limb S4 measured along radio-occultation paths, which reads higher than a ground receiver's S4. The COSMIC-2 satellites compute S4, a scintillation index, every 10 seconds for GPS signals that pass edge-on through the ionosphere below them. Each cell, about 840 km across, shows its highest hourly 95th percentile that UTC day; hover for the hour, the local solar time and how many values it rests on. Along the ray the place is uncertain by up to about 2,000 km. The shading steps are display choices: limb S4 is never compared with the alert levels used for ground S4. A dashed outline means most of that hour's values came while UCAR's interference index was raised, so interference, not the ionosphere, may have raised them. About 45°S to 45°N; only some COSMIC-2 satellites deliver these files, and the note says which. Checked against INGV ground scintillation receivers on 25 held-out days (validation, AUC 0.834). Full hourly files: the swx-data releases, CC BY 4.0, from UCAR COSMIC-2 (CDAAC).
  • Ground ROTI (Map options → Space weather): ROTI, a measured TEC fluctuation index at fixed stations, in TECU per minute. It measures how fast the electron content along each GPS and Galileo path changes, a proxy for ionospheric irregularities; it is not an S4 value and is never read against S4 alert levels. Each dot is an IGS station with its highest hourly 95th percentile that day; the dashed ring shows roughly where its paths above 30° cross 350 km. Hover for the hour and the station's other high hours. Irregularities at these latitudes usually follow sunset, so check the local solar time. Checked against INGV ground scintillation receivers on 25 held-out days (validation, AUC 0.887). Full hourly files: the swx-data releases, CC BY 4.0, from IGS station data via BKG (credit the IGS and the station operators).
  • Play (▶ beside the strip) steps through the days one by one; any other control stops it.
  • Cells are H3 resolution 4, about 1,770 km² each. A cell is coloured only when 5 or more aircraft were in it that day; 3–4 show as low coverage, and 1–2 are left out.
  • Thin coverage (grey diagonal hatching, on by default under Layers) marks areas about 300 km across (H3 resolution 2) where fewer than 10 of their 49 cells had 3 or more aircraft that day. Few aircraft were heard there, so the area can look quieter than it is. Areas with no cells at all are left blank. The same flag is published each day as <day>.coverage.json with the day's data files in the GitHub releases.
  • Share follows the formula GPSJam publishes: one bad aircraft is subtracted, so a single faulty receiver never colours a cell.
  • Severe GPS error means a reported position uncertainty of 93 m or worse (NACp below 8). "Live" leaves out aircraft still degraded after leaving interference elsewhere; tick "Also count aircraft still degraded…" under Map options → Measure to include them.
  • Accuracy counts only aircraft that showed 10 m accuracy at least once that day. Storms and SBAS outages show here, rarely on the 93 m measure.
  • Days to try: 2025-10-14 (EGNOS outage; accuracy measure over Europe), 2025-11-12 (G4 storm; accuracy measure over North America), 2026-09-22 (California GPS loss).

A daily, open picture of interference with satellite navigation and communications, from licence-clean sources. It measures effects, not causes, and no grade names a perpetrator.
