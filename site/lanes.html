Positioning, navigation and timing

Where GNSS receivers lose accuracy, are fed false positions, or are told to expect trouble, and how to tell deliberate interference from natural causes.

Validated

GNSS degradation from ADS-B

Phase 1

Share of aircraft per area whose own GNSS accuracy degraded, by day, hour and minute.

What

Aircraft broadcast their own position quality (NACp and NIC). The processor turns a day of worldwide ADS-B into the share of aircraft in each H3 hex (about 1,770 km²) that lost accuracy, at two thresholds: worse than 93 m (jamming-scale loss) and worse than 10 m for aircraft that showed 10 m capability that day (space weather and SBAS problems).

How

Version-2 reports from the aircraft's own GNSS only, airborne, sampled at most every 30 s. A peer test drops faulty avionics, which are bad where their neighbours are fine. A post-exposure tag separates aircraft still degraded after leaving a jammed area. The hex score uses the formula GPSJam publishes, and hexes are coloured only with 5 or more aircraft.

Data
  • adsb.lol daily archive (ODbL 1.0)
Notes

An effect measure, not attribution. Processing takes 53–70 s per worldwide day on one PC. Validated on 20 days (Oct–Nov 2025 and Sep 2026). Runs every day in GitHub Actions; history from 1 Oct 2025. Next: a cross-day blacklist of persistently faulty avionics, to complement the daily peer test.

Validated

SBAS outage and storm flags

Phase 1.2

Hours when an SBAS service area loses 10 m accuracy, labelled storm or probable service problem.

What

For each SBAS service area and UTC hour, the share of 10 m-capable aircraft that fell below 10 m. An hour is flagged as an outage at 60% or more and as degraded at 35% or more.

How

GFZ Kp of 5 or more labels a flagged hour as a storm; otherwise it is a probable service problem. Flagged hours are kept out of the 10 m baselines.

Data
  • adsb.lol daily archive
  • GFZ Potsdam Kp (CC BY 4.0)
Notes

Reconstructed the 13–17 Oct 2025 EGNOS outage to within an hour at both ends. On the 12 Nov 2025 G4 storm, 38.5% of capable aircraft fell below 10 m against a 3.7% baseline, while jamming-scale loss stayed flat. Planned: corroborate flags against EGNOS service notices and FAA WAAS NOTAMs.

Validated

Minute bursts and anomaly events

Phase 1.2

Short interference bursts timed to the minute, and departures from each area's own baseline.

What

Per-minute counts of aircraft with gross-bad reports catch bursts that an hourly majority rule would dampen. Each region and hour is also compared with the same hour on up to 14 earlier days.

How

An anomaly is a share above the median by more than the larger of a fixed floor and 4 × MAD, with at least 30 aircraft-hex-hours behind it.

Data
  • adsb.lol daily archive
Notes

Recovered both bursts of the 22 Sep 2026 San Francisco Bay Area GPS loss (18:00–18:08Z and 19:52–20:12Z). An FAA flight advisory confirms a Point Mugu Sea Range GPS test that day, 18:30–20:29Z; the second burst falls inside it.

Validated

Downstream effects by aircraft type

Validated (Oct 2026)

Which aircraft types stay degraded after leaving interference, and how far they carry it.

What

Some avionics keep reporting degraded accuracy for hours after an aircraft leaves a jammed or spoofed area. Over 17 days, A350-900s made up 24.9% of post-exposure aircraft-days against 1.3% of traffic (19.4×), A380s 19.2× and 777-300ERs 4.7×; most narrowbodies were near 1×.

How

An aircraft's exposure point is its first degraded hex-hour where its peers were degraded too. Later degraded reports 300 km or more away, among healthy peers, are tagged post-exposure.

Data
  • adsb.lol daily archive
Notes

Explained 23–26 Oct 2026 (verdicts 43 and 48). Inside jamming, A350s stay visible while degraded where other types go dark, and A350/A380 degraded episodes last 4–8 times longer. Australia reported to ICAO in June 2025 that on these types the ADS-B accuracy value is set to zero when one satellite is flagged degraded, so the type pattern is most likely a reporting artefact. It can't be removed by type without losing real detections (A350s and A380s also carry most real burst detections in hotspots), so the method stays v1.4: the type chart shows them apart and every daily table counts them, so each event says how much it rests on those types.

Parked

Radio-horizon jammer localizer

Parked (Oct 2026)

Narrow down where a jammer sits from where aircraft lose and regain GNSS.

What

A jammer on the ground can only reach aircraft above its radio horizon. Each aircraft's first jammed position and altitude define a horizon circle, and where many circles' edges meet points to the source area.

How

tools/horizon_fix.py fits the jammer's reach from the data and scores candidate cells by how tightly aircraft onsets agree. Scored against a 2025 TDOA network's fixes in Kaliningrad over two held-out sets: the best version is within 25 km of a known site on 3 of 5 days, but a single fixed point between the two sites does as well.

Data
  • adsb.lol daily archive
Notes

Parked 26 Oct 2026. Held-out tests (verdicts 41, 42 and 44) show it gives an area, not a site: two sites 21 km apart can't be told apart, and its likelihood version did no better than a constant guess. The code stays; revisit if a day with one known active site and time turns up. Positions stay off the public map.

Validated

ADS-B spoofing detection

Validated (Oct 2026)

Aircraft fed false positions: jumps, convergence on false points, altitude mismatches.

What

Spoofing shows up as position jumps, many aircraft converging on the same false point, and divergence between GNSS (geometric) and barometric altitude.

How

tools/spoof_day.py looks for positions an aircraft can't have flown: near-zero ground speed at altitude, staying in one spot at altitude, and jumps to a separate track and back. It doesn't rely on accuracy values: 61% of spoofing episodes kept good NACp. Over 22 days, the places aircraft were shown match published spoofing points (Beirut within 1 km, Amman 3 km, Moscow Sheremetyevo 2 km) and change when sources say they did; MLAT confirms 90% of the episodes it can test; six quiet control regions stay at 0.002% of aircraft-hours.

Data
  • adsb.lol daily archive
Notes

Validated 26 Oct 2026: a held-out test on 12 days fixed in advance passed all five pre-registered checks, first for version 0.4 and again for version 0.5, the default since late October. Version 0.4 adds a 'replay' class for feeds re-sending old positions (found over northern Florida, 6 Mar 2024). Version 0.5 lets indicated airspeed, from air data rather than GNSS, qualify an aircraft for the tests, so aircraft spoofed for their whole visible flight are tested: 13% more episodes over 41 days, controls unchanged at 0.002%. Two of the 12 held-out days were seen while designing 0.5, so that rerun is only partly independent. Spoofing and jamming are mostly separate signals. Maps show where receivers were told they were, not where a transmitter is. Offset tracks with no jump are missed; no coverage over Iraq and almost none over Iran. Not on the public map yet.

Validated

Spoofing at sea (AIS)

Validated, regional (Oct 2026)

Ship position reports that jump, circle or land ashore.

What

Ships broadcast GNSS-derived positions over AIS. Spoofed fixes appear as impossible tracks, ships placed on land and clusters in patterns.

How

A collector records Finland's open AIS stream (Digitraffic), and detectors flag impossible speeds, course swinging against a fixed heading, positions on land, and many ships reported on one spot. The same detectors also look for rendezvous, loitering, going dark and identity changes, for the maritime lane.

Data
  • Digitraffic AIS (CC BY 4.0)
  • Kystverket AIS (NLOD; licence text not yet read)
Notes

Validated 26 Oct 2026 for Finnish waters and the approaches, on a 24-hour capture held out and checked by rules set in advance: flagged ships were 0.13% in the control areas, 97% of those with a trusted position were in the eastern Gulf of Finland (140 times the control rate), and the aircraft map agreed (22 times). The ships' own lost-fix reports agreed less than set in advance (2.6 times against 3). Flagged ships peak at night, and the circle in inland Russia (Tver region) that their receivers were moved to on 24 Oct was used again by 19 ships. The flags point to GNSS spoofing of the ships' receivers rather than false AIS. Coverage is regional: Finland's open AIS only.

Declared interference and test notices

Phase 2

What authorities say: announced GPS interference tests now; navigation warnings and NOTAMs next.

What

Official notices that GNSS may be unreliable in an area and time window: maritime navigation warnings, US GPS interference test notices, NOTAMs and announced test events.

How

The FAA's public flight advisories for US GPS interference tests are read every morning (window, centre and radius by altitude). An anomaly or burst inside a test's window and reach is tagged on the map as consistent with an announced test, rather than counted as an incident. Other notices will join the same layer.

Emitters seen from space

Transmitters that show up in satellite radar data as interference inside the radar's own band.

Partly validated

L-band emitters from NISAR radar

Phase 7 (pulled forward)

Transmitters inside NISAR's L-band receive slices (one contains GPS L2), from the processor's own RFI tables.

What

NISAR's L-band radar listens in 1219–1239 MHz (contains GPS L2) and 1291–1296 MHz over most land. Its processor records, for every block of raw data, how much interference it found and at which frequencies, before filtering it out.

How

Two detectors run on those tables. Tones: frequencies hit in most pulses, excluding the radar's own two tones. Broadband: the cross-polarized channel far above what the ground produces, the signature of a noise jammer. Positions come from the orbit, and a cheap first pass on the processor's score decides which passes get the full read.

Data
  • NISAR L1 RSLC and L2 GCOV via ASF DAAC (NASA data, CC0 unless marked)
Notes

The broadband detector passed its first positive control: an area with documented L1/L2/L5 jamming was detected on 12 of 13 passes that covered it. The published Shiraz jammer was not confirmed by either detector, and the earlier image-based method failed that test. Outputs stay private until validated. Credit to @auonsson, whose post prompted the idea.

Parked

Sentinel-1 RFI board

Parked (Oct 2026)

C-band emitters from the interference reports in Sentinel-1 radar products.

What

Sentinel-1 Level-1 products carry per-burst radio-frequency-interference annotations. Mapped over time, they show persistent and new C-band emitters.

How

Reads only the RFI reports in each Sentinel-1 product (a few hundred kB per pass, never the imagery). Each burst report is placed on the ground from the product's geolocation grid, and passes from different orbits are combined on one grid. A site is where most viewing directions agree, persistently across passes.

Data
  • Copernicus Sentinel-1 L1 GRD annotations via Microsoft Planetary Computer (free, including commercial use; attribution)
Notes

Parked 26 Oct 2026. Run privately on three published cases and a quiet control: the control stayed silent and region-scale C-band interference shows clearly, but its sites didn't line up with the published radar locations, because the reports place an emitter only to tens of kilometres. Revisit when a known C-band emitter's position is published. Outputs stay private. No account needed.

Parked

Weather-radar spokes

Parked (Oct 2026)

Interference spokes in open weather-radar data, as evidence of nearby emitters.

What

A strong emitter in a weather radar's band paints a thin radial spoke on its scans. National services publish the scans.

How

tools/radar_spokes.py reads the unfiltered lowest sweep from German (DWD) and Finnish (FMI) radars, finds rays whose range-corrected power stays level along range (the signature of a transmitter, not weather), separates the sun and rain, groups spokes into persistent sources and crosses bearings between radars. First day (23 Oct 2026, 29 radars): 101 sources, the sun found to within 0.23°, and bearing crossings no better than chance, so no emitter located yet. A second day (24 Oct) found all 10 persistent sources again within 1°.

Data
  • FMI radar (CC BY 4.0)
  • DWD radar (CC BY 4.0)
Notes

Parked 26 Oct 2026. Most sources are steady, narrow, weak and seen by one radar only, which matches published descriptions of outdoor Wi-Fi links in the band: a pattern, not an identification, and nothing points to military activity. With no validation target it waits for a specific case. C-band, so it complements the L-band NISAR lane.

Validated

Spaceborne and network GNSS

Validated: CYGNSS (Oct 2026)

GNSS reflectometry, radio occultation, ground reference networks and L-band radiometers.

What

CYGNSS GNSS-reflectometry has been used to localize a jammer (the Shiraz case). COSMIC-2 radio-occultation amplitude can indicate ground jamming power. CORS/IGS ground stations record degradation at fixed sites, and SMOS/SMAP map 1.4 GHz interference.

How

CYGNSS first: the noise floor in each delay-Doppler map, against a same-day baseline for the same spacecraft, antenna and land/sea mix (tools/cygnss_rfi.py). On six days it separated aircraft-jammed from aircraft-clean areas with AUC 0.98; on 21 held-out days, with the method frozen and the checks set in advance, AUC 0.97, 99% of jammed cells caught at 1 dB, and control areas quiet. Over a full year (383 days) the AUC is 0.974. About 80% of the areas it flags have no aircraft coverage, such as the Red Sea, Iran and Myanmar. Ground reference stations: tools/station_cn0.py compares each EPN/IGS station's GPS L1 C/A C/N0 with its own quiet days at the same sky position and flags simultaneous drops on most satellites. First test (southern Baltic; 6 stations 110–486 km from the Kaliningrad coast; 6 jammed days, 2 controls): no flags on any of 48 station-days, while aircraft within 150 km were degraded. Coastal ground jammers are beyond these stations' line of sight, so the null was expected; the next test needs stations within sight of an emitter.

Data
  • NASA CYGNSS
  • UCAR COSMIC-2
  • EPN and IGS stations via BKG (CC BY 4.0); NOAA CORS
  • ESA SMOS, NASA SMAP (licences UNVERIFIED for these products)
Notes

CYGNSS validated 26 Oct 2026 on its pre-registered held-out test: all six checks hold on 21 unseen days (pooled AUC 0.974, 98.7% of jammed cells caught at 1 dB, 15.8% false alarms, at least 0.959 with each region left out). The separate check on announced US GPS tests came out "not shown" (AUC 0.57 against the 0.70 set in advance), although some tests are seen with the published timing. Location is coarse: the antenna beam is a few hundred km wide, so the map says where satellites were exposed, not where a transmitter is. Coverage is within about 38° of the equator (no Baltic). Not on the public map yet. The ground-station part is still a prototype; a station sees a ground jammer only within roughly 35–60 km (line of sight). Licences are checked before any other product here feeds a layer.

Running

Sun outages: calculator, board and alerts

Running (from Oct 2026)

Predictable daily losses when the sun passes behind a GEO satellite, to the minute, with alerts when observed solar conditions make them worse.

What

Twice a year the sun sits behind each GEO satellite as seen from a given receiver, and dishes lose the signal for a few minutes a day. Losses inside those windows are natural; losses outside them deserve a second look. A strong solar radio burst can black out sun-facing links, and GNSS on the sunlit side, at any time.

How

A low-precision solar ephemeris (Meeus, about 0.01°), an ideal geostationary satellite and a WGS84 receiver; an outage is the sun within half the dish beamwidth plus the sun's radius of the satellite (1.2° for a 0.9 m Ku-band dish). The Sun page computes windows for any place, slot and dish in the browser; the home page predicts them live for 32 slots seen from 36 cities and sets them against NOAA's live solar radio flux and radio bursts. An alert fires when a predicted outage meets observed evidence, or when a strong L-band burst is observed; the feed carries them.

Data
  • Computed geometry
  • NOAA SWPC solar radio flux, radio bursts and alerts (US public domain)
  • Curated public outage reports (cited)
Notes

The browser code matches the project's Python tool on six pairs in both hemispheres, and the Python tool matched an independently written calculator for Tehran. Alert thresholds are provisional. Our own aircraft data joins the alerts once a check against archived radio bursts passes. The planned interference register now lives here, as the curated outage-reports file (public sources, each cited).

Ships and the signals around them: what radar satellites see that AIS doesn't, behaviour that suggests hiding or pressure, and emitters at sea.

Prototype

Vessels, dark ships and signals at sea

Prototype (M2 scored, Oct 2026)

Find vessels that hide, transfer cargo at sea or press others near contested features, and the emitters they carry, starting in the South China Sea and Indian Ocean.

What

Combines ships seen by radar satellites while silent on AIS (dark ships), AIS tracks with no ship under them (phantom tracks), ship-to-ship transfers, identity changes, spoofed tracks, behaviour over undersea cables, RF emitters at sea and HF signal metadata, with weather and declared activity as context.

How

First one method, validated and written up: Sentinel-1 ship detections matched against AIS at one focus area. Ship detection is built and runs privately on the first area (19 radar passes in 60 days). Checked against Sentinel-2 optical images, about half of its detections are confirmed at the best setting, against 13–31% at the first one. Matched against Finland's open AIS at the radar's acquisition time on a held-out pass (26 Oct 2026, pre-registered): all 75 ships of 100 m or more were found, with a 105 m median gap. With OpenStreetMap coastlines, 3 of 86 large detections had no AIS match (1 after a map of fixed objects), so the amended setting is validated for Finnish waters on one pass; the pre-registered setting, with Natural Earth coastlines, only partly (islets swamp it). A seven-day extension is running. Then behaviour detectors, context layers and a separate Sea map with licence-clean layers only.

Data
  • Digitraffic AIS (CC BY 4.0)
  • Official sanctions lists (OFAC SDN, UK Sanctions List)
  • Copernicus Sentinel-1 and -2
  • VIIRS night lights (licence to confirm)
  • AIS (aisstream, private until its terms are clear)
  • Declared warnings
  • Public HF receivers (metadata only)
Notes

A public Sea map shows sanctioned, military, law-enforcement and Russian, Chinese, Iranian or North Korean-flagged vessels in Finland's open AIS, with official listings, reporting patterns and failed spoofing tests; everything else stays private. No open AIS feed covers the South China Sea or Indian Ocean, and no free source sees per-vessel satellite communications. Communications content is never published.

Context and assessment

Turning lane outputs into graded events with a stated basis, and keeping natural and benign explanations in view.

Running

Space-weather context

Phase 1–2

Geomagnetic storms and ionospheric scintillation, to separate natural degradation from deliberate interference.

What

Storms and scintillation degrade GNSS too. The Kp index already labels SBAS flags. ESA's worldwide scintillation nowcast (S4, sigma-phi and TEC on a 2.5° grid) has been archived since 21 Oct 2026, because ESA keeps only the current hour. So far about one hour in five is kept, because GitHub runs the capture only every few hours. The events grader reads it (rule N3).

How

Context layers sit beside the observed lanes. Thresholds follow ICAO's scintillation categories (S4 0.5/0.8, sigma-phi 0.4/0.7 rad).

Data
  • GFZ Potsdam Kp (CC BY 4.0)
  • ESA Space Weather Service Network scintillation nowcast (ESA S2P terms)
  • NOAA SWPC and GloTEC (US public domain)
Notes

The ESA terms allow derived products with attribution, but the raw grids may be passed on only to people who accept the same terms, so the archive itself stays private. 'Data provided by the European Space Agency (ESA) as part of its Space Safety Programme (S2P)'.

Built

Events and grading

Phase 6 (v1.1, 27 Oct 2026)

Fusing lanes into events, each graded on five fields.

What

An event joins what the lanes saw in one place and time window. It is graded on signal type, support, localization, attribution and verdict.

How

tools/events.py turns lane outputs into observations (ADS-B anomalies, bursts, all-day jamming-scale regions and SBAS flags; CYGNSS noise-floor cells; FAA announced GPS tests; GFZ Kp storms; curated official notices; EASA's weekly FIR tables) and fuses them per UTC day: same or adjacent H3 res-2 region, overlapping or adjacent hours, no two members more than 600 km apart. Exclusion checks (announced test, geomagnetic storm, ionospheric scintillation, official notice) set the verdict. Every grade stores its rule and the observations behind it.

Data
  • All lanes
Notes

Rules frozen as v1.0 on 26 Oct 2026; v1.1 (27 Oct) adds EASA's FIR tables as a source for 'deliberate_suspected'. They are stated choices, not yet validated. 1 Oct 2025 – 25 Oct 2026: about 160,000 events. 8,632 reach 'Multiple sources' (an aircraft 93 m signal and a raised CYGNSS noise field agreeing within 300 km). 691 effect events are 'planned': inside an announced FAA test window and its FL250 radius, the one the map draws. 905 are 'benign_natural' (10 m-only effects at storm time). 1,322 are 'deliberate_suspected': EASA's weekly table listed spoofing in the event's FIR that week (FIRs approximated by country outlines). EASA's tables exist for only 13 weeks of the period (the current one and Internet Archive copies), so that count follows which weeks have a table, not how much spoofing there was. NGA's warning feed has been frozen since May 2024, so other notices are added by hand, each cited; the two on file (Baltic, Gulf of Finland) say 'interference', so they corroborate without changing a verdict. The scintillation rule reads ESA's nowcast, archived since 21 Oct 2026. Only monthly counts are published (Validation page); the register stays private. Grades describe evidence strength. They don't name perpetrators. Known look-alikes, such as radar faults, are kept here as test cases (this absorbed the planned exclusion-layer lane).

Public map, charts and data

Phase 2

The map updates every day from an automatic pipeline; the charts cover the validated days.

What

A static site: a Leaflet map of H3 hexes, charts, the method card and the validation report. Each day's hex data is downloadable under ODbL, and the full daily outputs sit in monthly data releases.

How

A GitHub Actions workflow processes each new adsb.lol day after it is published (about 03:30 UTC the next day), stores the outputs as assets on a monthly GitHub release, and redeploys the site. The home page is a live status board (alerts, sun outages, solar radio, GNSS today, sea), which replaced the planned daily-status product; a weekly assessment will come from the same data.
