    Results so far

    • Hotspots reproduce. The known Baltic, Black Sea and Caucasus hotspots appear on every test day; quiet control regions stay near zero.
    • An SBAS outage, from aircraft alone. The 13–17 Oct 2025 EGNOS outage was reconstructed to within an hour at both ends.
    • Storms separate from jamming. On the 12 Nov 2025 G4 storm, 38.5% of capable aircraft lost 10 m accuracy against a 3.7% baseline, while jamming-scale loss stayed flat.
    • Minute-level timing. Both bursts of the 22 Sep 2026 Bay Area GPS loss were recovered.
    • Downstream effects by type. A350-900s are 19× over-represented among aircraft still degraded after leaving interference. Why is still a hypothesis.
    • L-band emitters from radar. NISAR's own interference tables detected an area of documented L1/L2/L5 jamming on 12 of 13 passes. The published Shiraz jammer was not confirmed.

    How it works

    • Method first. Frozen test days, a published method card and validation before any user interface.
    • Licence-clean. Every source is recorded with its licence before it feeds a layer; non-commercial sources are for visual comparison only. See the registry.
    • Static and free. Daily batch jobs write small files served from a CDN: no servers, no database bill.
    • Effects, not attribution. Every output says what was observed and on what basis.
    • Public and private kept apart. Watch areas and alerts never enter the public repo or this site.
