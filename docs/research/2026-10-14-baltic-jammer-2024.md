# Locating the 2024 Baltic GNSS jammer: open-source work by @auonsson and collaborators

Research of 14 Oct 2026 (Claude research agent; sources below). It covers methods and evidence, for reproduction in Noisefloor. Anything that couldn't be confirmed is marked **UNVERIFIED**.

The analysis threads were posted on X. Many were later imported to Bluesky with their original dates (`auonsson.bsky.social`) and were read there through the public API. The Mastodon account has been inactive since August 2023. **B:xxx** below means `https://bsky.app/profile/auonsson.bsky.social/post/xxx`.

## Timeline

- **15 Dec 2023:** aircraft over the south Baltic start losing GNSS. Space weather was ruled out using Swepos ionosphere data [B:3lboflzoq5k2q]. By 30 Dec, maps of NIC 1–6 reports in 33 km hexes suggested about three jammers or target areas [B:3lbod65el7q2e].
- **3 Jan 2024, proof 1:** a 300 km disc around each degraded position (about 27k positions). The area where the discs overlapped most was Kaliningrad [B:3lbocew5ouq2p].
- **4 Jan, proof 2:** a larger dataset, keeping only hours when the jammer was on (281 or more bad positions an hour). Again Kaliningrad [B:3lbocrcp2ye22, B:3lbodzfzvgb2q].
- **14 Jan:** a live collector starts, polling once a minute [B:3lbo5wixijx2e].
- **15 Feb:** a Substack post separates a single GPS satellite (PRN27) outage from the jamming.
- **18–24 Mar:** the footprint changed. First a NW sector was spared, then it covered almost only the Warsaw FIR, which suggests a directional emitter. 1,614 aircraft were affected in 47 h [B:3lbnpml3idq2q, B:3lbnnnksuil2r].
- **30 Mar, proof 3:** altitude-based radio horizons from each aircraft's **first jammed position** (Feb–Mar data, 47,378 points filtered to 11,496). Result: Kaliningrad, probably on the coast [B:3lbnmflwdgx2m].
- **2 Apr, proof 4:** intersections of the horizon circle edges. The source looked stationary and on land [B:3lbnmsmsah42a].
- **6 Apr, proof 5:** @PajalaJussi counted circle intersections in 10×10 km cells. The top Kaliningrad cells had more than 60k against a mean of about 4k, clustered on the central cape [B:3lbnijb3vhv2e, B:3lbnk2kaioz2w].
- **29 Apr:** the jammer affecting Estonia was placed roughly midway between Narva and St Petersburg [B:3lbnbt5fuka2i]. On 9 Aug it was reported to have moved NW of St Petersburg [B:3lbmjo46dfs2m].
- **Later in 2024:** more than 50k flights jammed since December [B:3lbmyo75ux62w]. In December, spoofing from Kaliningrad was reported too [B:3lecu5imhn226].
- **Stated precision:** confident about the country, not about an exact site [B:3lbnjc3mmbd23]. No specific site was named.

## Methods

**Detection.**
- Each ADS-B message with NIC 1–6 counts as degraded. NIC 0 was excluded at first as ambiguous; a 3 Mar revision showed NIC 0 and track dropouts mean undercounting.
- The main metric was the per-minute percentage of aircraft affected in a box.
- Later maps added NACp < 6 and kept only airliners (emitter categories A2–A6).

**Range-disc overlap (proofs 1, 3).** Draw a disc around each degraded position (a fixed 300 km at first, later the aircraft's radio horizon). The jammer should be where most discs overlap.

**Horizon-edge intersection (proofs 4, 5).** The jammer lies on the radio-horizon circle of each aircraft's first-jammed position. The method, as published in the collaborator's code:
- radius r = 4.12·(√h_tx + √h_rx) km, with heights in metres and the transmitter at ground level
- coordinates projected to UTM 33N
- pairwise circle intersections, kept only for crossing angles between about 50° and 130°
- an optional filter keeping intersections ahead of the aircraft's heading
- counts in 10 km cells

**Supporting evidence.** Onset timing (switch-on and switch-off), footprint shape, a historic-reach polygon used to filter false positives, RINEX SNR drops at Bornholm and south Sweden ground stations, and AIS and NAVTEX checks. There was no RF direction-finding.

## Corroboration

- **Schäfer et al., "Jammer on the Horizon" (ION GNSS+ 2023):** the same radio-horizon principle, published earlier.
- **Gattis, Cydejko & Akos, GPS Solutions 30:96 (2026), CC BY:** a TDOA network on the Polish coast, Apr–Jun 2025. It placed a jammer and a circular spoofer in Kaliningrad, one fix at Baltiysk harbour, and noted reception beyond the geometric horizon (ducting or refraction).
- **Defense News, 2 Jul 2025:** Okunevo antenna site and SW of Baltiysk, about 1 km precision. It describes the heatmap work.
- **Spire:** attributed a 14 Oct 2024 event to a vessel NW of Kaliningrad.
- **Media:** Newsweek (4 Jan and 25 Mar 2024), ERR/Iltalehti (5 Jan 2024), CEPA (30 Apr 2024). New Scientist coverage is **UNVERIFIED** (the page couldn't be fetched).
- **Official:**
  - Estonian and Lithuanian foreign ministers blamed Russia (Apr 2024).
  - Eight states filed a UN complaint (2025).
  - Lithuania's regulator reported 36 spoofing antennas (2026).
  - No official statement named a site. A Finnish Traficom statement of May 2024 blaming Russia is **UNVERIFIED**.

## For Noisefloor

**What it established.** Several independent lines point to Kaliningrad as the main south-Baltic source: these ADS-B methods, government statements, and the 2025 TDOA fixes. Site-level fixes came only from TDOA.

**Limitations of the ADS-B methods.**
- They assume an omnidirectional, full-power emitter at ground level with standard refraction. Directional behaviour and ducting break that.
- Multiple or ship-borne emitters blur the result.
- Coverage bias: most traffic is NW of Kaliningrad.
- NIC 0 reports and dropouts lead to undercounting.
- There is no uncertainty estimate.
- Prefer barometric altitude: geometric altitude is GNSS-derived.

**Automatable on adsb.lol (ODbL):**
1. On/off events and duty cycle from the existing per-minute counts against a baseline.
2. A **radio-horizon localizer.** For each aircraft, record its first-degraded and recovery positions with barometric altitude. Compute horizon circles, pairwise intersections with an angle filter, and counts in H3 or 10 km cells, accumulated daily. This reuses the per-aircraft exposure tracking added for the v1.4 post-exposure tag. Validate against the 2025 TDOA fixes and the Okunevo/Baltiysk report.
3. Spoofing flags: position jumps, NACp collapse, circular tracks (Phase 4).
